# Word choice and international style

Use these rules as audience and meaning checks. They are not a universal
thesaurus or a ban list.

## Microsoft

Source: <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/word-choice/avoid-jargon>

- Use familiar words when they preserve the meaning.
- Keep jargon when it is the shortest precise term for the intended audience.
- Spell out an acronym when the audience may not know it. Keep established API,
  protocol, product, and command names unchanged.
- Replace business or marketing jargon such as leverage when it only means use.
- Judge the whole sentence, not a word in isolation.

## Federal plain language

Sources:

- <https://plainlanguage.gov/guidelines/>
- <https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language>
- <https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles>
- <https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/writing>

Name the audience and the action. Put the responsible actor near the verb when
responsibility matters. Prefer direct verbs, present tense for instructions,
and an order that follows the reader's task. Passive voice is correct when the
actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally omitted. Do not force English
voice rules onto German, Polish, French, or Japanese grammar.

## Apple international style

Sources:

- <https://help.apple.com/applestyleguide/>
- <https://support.apple.com/guide/applestyleguide/welcome/web>
- <https://support.apple.com/guide/applestyleguide/intro-to-international-style-apsg1ff68ab5/1.0/web/1.0>
- <https://support.apple.com/guide/applestyleguide/languages-apsg1ff6873c/1.0/web/1.0>

Use simple sentence structures, avoid idioms and colloquialisms, and use
international conventions for dates, times, numbers, units, countries, and
languages. Preserve locale-correct diacritics and punctuation. Apply Apple's
terminology authority to Apple products and documentation; do not translate a
shell command, API identifier, or package name.

## Replacement order

1. State the reader's action.
2. Remove a word that adds only status or mood.
3. Replace a familiar-word mismatch with the plain term.
4. Keep a technical term when it carries precision.
5. Name the property, mechanism, source, number, or constraint that justifies
   a stronger word.
